Our story

Under Mama Yene’s umbrella

We met in our second year at university, under a friend’s POS umbrella. Shout out to Mama Yene, who owned the umbrella and the POS. We weren’t friends then—just two people sheltering from the same rain. Not long after, the friend left school, and the umbrella went with her.

We didn’t speak again until final year. He was finishing a four-year chemistry degree; I was in the fifth year of chemical engineering. We were both in our respective final years, but we might as well have been on different campuses for all we saw of each other.

Then he called. It was a health crisis—his—and he needed someone to talk to. We spoke that day, and the next, and the next. When school resumed, we kept talking. We kept hanging out. We were always together, walking the same routes, weathering the same days.

He said “I love you” first. I didn’t say it back immediately, but I didn’t stop thinking about it. We’ve been officially together since 2023, though we’ve known each other since 2020.

Now we’re getting married in Eket. The traditional rites on Thursday, the white wedding on Saturday at eight in the morning, and the reception at noon. Two families, two rivers, one sea.
