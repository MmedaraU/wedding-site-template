The Long Read · Lagos

How a borrowed umbrella
led to a marriage

It began, as these things do, in Lagos traffic — the kind that turns a short trip into a long meditation. Tunde had borrowed an umbrella from a stranger at the Yaba bus stop, and Adunni had watched him hand it back with the solemnity of a man returning a relic.

They were both twenty-six and neither was looking for anything in particular. She was between flats in Lekki. He was between opinions about what to do with a degree in something impractical. The umbrella lasted four more blocks before it gave up entirely, and by then they had already decided to share a cab to Surulere.

Nine years later the umbrella is gone, the flat is not, and the cab has become a standing arrangement. What follows is an account of the day they intend to make it official — first at the family compound in Surulere, then at The Yard in Victoria Island — set down here for the benefit of friends, family, and anyone who has ever been rained on at an opportune moment.

The ceremony will be short. The owambe will not be. There will be a band, and there will be jollof, and there will be a point at which the band stops, and what happens after that is between the guests and the lagoon.

The Particulars

Order of the day

01

The Ìgbéyàwó

Friday, at the family compound, Surulere.

The traditional wedding. Aso oke, family, and more food than is strictly reasonable. Arrive from four. Bring your dancing shoes and your appetite. Ẹ káàbọ̀.

02

The White Wedding

Saturday, at The Yard, Victoria Island.

Half past two, by the water. Seating is unassigned, the floor is uneven, and the December breeze is not our friend. Wear something you can stand in for twenty minutes.

03

The Dress Code

Aso ebi available. Comfort is mandatory.

Aso ebi is available in the family colours. Black tie is welcome. So is the agbada you have owned for a decade. There are no rules beyond this one: no florals. We mean it.
