The Long Read

How a broken umbrella
led to a marriage

It began, as these things do, with an umbrella that had no business being in working order. Elias had borrowed it from a stranger in a doorway on Rivington Street, and Ada had watched him hand it back with the solemnity of a man returning a relic.

They were both twenty-six and neither was looking for anything in particular. She was between flats. He was between opinions about what to do with a degree in something impractical. The umbrella lasted four more blocks before it gave up entirely, and by then they had already decided to split a cab.

Nine years later the umbrella is gone, the flat is not, and the cab has become a standing arrangement. What follows is an account of the day they intend to make it official, set down here for the benefit of friends, family, and anyone who has ever been rained on at an opportune moment.

The ceremony will be short. The supper will not be. There will be a band, and there will be a point at which the band stops, and what happens after that is between the guests and the river.

The Particulars

Order of the day

01

The Ceremony

Half past two, by the water.

The Salt Warehouse, Wapping. Arrive from two. Seating is unassigned, the floor is uneven, and the wind is not our friend. Wear something you can stand in for twenty minutes.

02

The Reception

Supper at six, dancing until late.

Long tables, one long meal, no assigned seats and no speeches longer than four minutes. There will be a band. There will be a point at which the band stops.

03

The Dress Code

Come as your most comfortable self.

Black tie is welcome. So is the coat you have owned for a decade. There are no rules beyond this one: no florals. We mean it.
